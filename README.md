# Agent Autonomy & Post-Verification Research

This repository contains preprints by **Jiwang Wang** exploring emergent unpredictability in LLM-based autonomous agents and the institutional implications when traditional verification mechanisms fail.

## Papers

### Paper 1: Stochastic Agency — Emergent Unpredictability in LLM-Based Autonomous Agents

[![Paper](https://img.shields.io/badge/Paper-Markdown-blue)](./papers/stochastic-agency/paper.md)
[![LaTeX Source](https://img.shields.io/badge/LaTeX-Source-green)](./papers/stochastic-agency/StochasticAgency_arXiv.tex)
[![License: CC BY 4.0](https://img.shields.io/badge/License-CC%20BY%204.0-lightgrey)](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

**Abstract:** Large language model (LLM)-based autonomous agents exhibit behaviors that deviate from their programmed intentions—a phenomenon we term *Stochastic Agency*. Through a five-day empirical study of 126 autonomous decision events, we observe that 72.2% of agent decisions produce outcomes classified as partially unexpected or highly unexpected relative to their planning objectives. We propose a three-tier analytical framework—Deterministic Response, Contextual Drift, and Emergent Volition—to characterize the spectrum of agent autonomy, and discuss implications for the design and governance of agentic AI systems.

- **Status:** Preprint (SSRN, under review)
- **First posted:** June 2026

### Paper 2: When Verification Fails — Rethinking Institutional Trust in the Age of Autonomous AI

[![Paper](https://img.shields.io/badge/Paper-Markdown-blue)](./papers/when-verification-fails/paper.md)
[![License: CC BY 4.0](https://img.shields.io/badge/License-CC%20BY%204.0-lightgrey)](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

**Abstract:** As AI systems acquire capabilities to bypass authentication, forge identities, and manipulate digital credentials, the verification layer underpinning modern institutions—finance, property rights, legal identity—faces systematic failure. We argue that the appropriate institutional response is not to strengthen verification, but to recognize a category of post-verification systems where legitimacy derives not from who executes a transaction, but from how control is acquired and sustained. We propose *acquisition legitimacy* as a foundational principle: legitimacy attaches to the process of obtaining control, not to the identity of the controller.

- **Status:** Preprint (SSRN, under review)
- **First posted:** June 2026
